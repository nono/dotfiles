## Problem

[sc-5033](https://app.shortcut.com/template-systems/story/5033): the scheduler sends a Lifty to fetch a tote before a
pick module is free. When the order has no pick station, `sendLiftyToPickModule` stamped the station, and so gave the
order its wave slot, only after it found a route to a module. On the GXO runs the only free module was often still
held by the Lifty that had just picked, so the route failed. The fetch was committed, but the order held no slot.
Other orders filled the wave while the Lifty waited, and the sc-4970 rule sent the tote back in unpicked: 7–8 % of
fetches.

## Change

- `holdWaveSlotForDelivery` (order.go) stamps the order and applies the future-wave gate **before** the route. The
  station is already fixed at that point: `pickStationModuleIDs` returns the modules of one station, and it now also
  returns that station's ID. A failed route leaves the order stamped, with its slot, and the carrier keeps the tote.
- A stamp is never moved, so it must not name a station the carrier can never reach. Before the stamp, a BFS over
  the impassable cells checks that the carrier can reach a module of the station. A Lifty in the way, even one that
  cannot move this run, does not count: the carrier waits for it.
- sc-4970's rule is unchanged: when no wave can admit the order, nothing is stamped and the tote is stacked in.

## Not in this PR

A Quint model of the change found two gaps that the merge base already has. Both still let a fetched tote be stacked
back in:

- Admission counts stamped orders only. A slot can go to another order between the fetch assignment and the fetch
  commit, for example while the first Lifty waits on `MustWaitUntilWayIsClear`.
- `pickStationModuleIDs` needs a module that no Lifty has reserved. When every module of a station with a free slot
  is reserved at the fetch commit, the order is not stamped.

Also out of scope: sc-4997 (a soft give-up ends the delivery pass) and the "already carried by another agent" log
after a failed route.
